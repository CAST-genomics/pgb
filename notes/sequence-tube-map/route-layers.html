What this is

A tube map draws 464 strands and they cannot be told apart. At every branch the layout reorders them, so a strand's neighbours keep changing and where it sits tells you nothing about which strand it is.

Depth Cueing for Route Disambiguation — the proposal throughout this document — suggests ordering routes in depth. That only works if routes are separable. This app tests whether they are, and tests nothing else. It computes the routes in one document and writes each one as its own SVG.

No depth cue is built. No PGB code is changed.

01

Vocabulary

Seven words. The first four are CONTEXT.md's; the last three name the parts of the document read as a table. They are fixed for the rest of this document, and no other word is used for any of them.

The whole document is the inside of one node. What the document calls a node is called a segment here, so that it is never confused with PGB's node.

Reading the document as a table

A strand passes through at most one segment in a column. A cell shows which segment of that column the strand passes through, or that it passes through none.

A route is one strand's complete traversal — every segment it passes through, from one end of the document to the other. Strands that pass through the same segments have the same route.

02

Finding the routes

A route is built from one fact repeated 274 times: which strands pass through this segment. That fact cannot come from PGB's data. The document is one PGB node, and PGB has no names for the 274 segments inside it. It has to come from the drawing.

So it is taken from the drawing and then checked against the drawing. Two independent readings:

the box

A segment is drawn as a box whose height is 15 × strands + 18: 15 units for every strand that passes through it, plus 9 units of corner at each end. The box states its own count.

the strands

Each strand's upper edge is rebuilt from the drawing — rectangles as they are, curves flattened — and sampled at the box's midpoint. A strand that falls inside the box passes through the segment.

A first version read only the rectangles and agreed on 176 of 274. Every miss was a 1 bp segment, which is drawn as a curve and never as a rectangle. Without the count, every single-base difference would have been missing from every route.

Nothing lost in the split. 40,442 drawn elements in the document, 40,442 across the 112 route files, 464 strands, none repeated.

04

The app

112 layers, one per route, drawn on top of each other. Five controls.

route list

One line per route, with a bar for how many strands have it.

click to solo

Draws one route at full strength and fades the other 111.

checkbox

Shows or hides any layer, so any set of routes can be drawn together.

zoom

The document is 108,982 by 7,785 units. Drawn to fit, a strand is thinner than one pixel, so zoom is needed to see anything.

casing · shadow

The two treatments the proposal suggests, wired up so they can be looked at.

The result

The layers separate. One route drawn alone is legible where all 112 together are not, and any set of them can be drawn together without moving anything.

05

Two problems with ordering routes

The proposal offers two ways to order routes in depth: by how many strands have a route, and by how far a route differs from the most common one. It ranks the second first, and says the two are close enough to substitute. On this document they are not.

1 · The two orders are unrelated

spearman(strands, difference) = +0.104   p = 0.27

That is no relationship. The two orders put the routes in different depths, so the choice between them changes the result.

2 · A missing strand looks like a different one

Difference is measured by how much two routes overlap. Five routes, with 95 strands between them, share almost nothing with the most common route. None of them is a different path through the document. Each passes through 5 to 12 segments, against the most common route's 179, and the two ends of the document are where those segments are.

These strands are not different from the most common route; they are absent from this part of the document. Ordering by difference brings them to the front as the most unusual thing in the picture. Whichever order is chosen, difference has to be measured over the segments a strand could have passed through.

One document. Every number here is from 5520+. Whether either problem appears elsewhere is not measured.

06

Strata, removed

The first version of this app put the 112 routes into five groups by how many strands they have, because the proposal notes that a reader can only see about five depths.

That is true of a depth cue, but wrong for this app. The app exists to separate routes, and grouping ninety-six of them together joined back up most of what the split had taken apart.

It could not have worked at this document in any case. Seventy of the 112 routes have one strand each, so any grouping by count has to put all seventy in one group. Moving the boundaries changed that group from 108 routes to 96.

One route, one layer. Grouping belongs to whatever draws the depth cue.

07

Limits

  • No depth cue was judged. Casing and shadow are wired up and both do something. Whether either reads as depth at this thickness is not answered here.
  • Nothing renders in PGB. These are SVGs written to disk. src/tubemap/ is unchanged and PGB computes no routes.
  • Depth surviving a pan is untested. That needs routes computed live, which do not exist.
  • One document, one node. These routes are inside 5520+. They say nothing about routes across nodes, which is the scale the proposal's third caveat is about.
