Route Layers

A tube map document taken apart along route boundaries and written back out as one SVG per route — 112 files, 112 layers, stackable in any order. Built to answer one question before any renderer is written: are routes separable objects at all?

Why it exists

A tube map of 464 strands is a mat. You cannot follow one strand across it, and the reason is structural: at every branch the layout re-orders the strands to minimise crossings, so a strand's neighbours change constantly and position carries no identity.

Depth Cueing for Route Disambiguation proposed a way out — order routes in depth, because a route is the only object in the picture that can hold a consistent order across the whole width. The argument is good. It also rests on an assumption nobody had checked: that routes exist as separable objects in a real document, in useful numbers, and that pulling one out leaves something legible behind.

This app checks that assumption and nothing else. It computes routes offline and writes each one as its own drawable, so they can be stacked, hidden, and lifted out by hand. No depth cue is implemented. No renderer is touched.

Deliberately upstream. Every question about how depth should look is downstream of whether the objects separate. That is the only question answered here.

01

Four words, used strictly

The vocabulary is CONTEXT.md's and the terms are not interchangeable. Two of them collide with the server's own spelling, which is the whole reason the aliases exist.

TermIsHereServer calls it
nodegraph vertex PGB draws in 3Dexactly one — 5520+—
segmenta sequence box inside a node274node
strandone haplotype's path464track
routethe set of segments a strand traverses112—

The whole document is the inside of one node. There is no second node anywhere in it. The boxes that look like nodes are segments — the server spells them <g class="node">, and PGB renames them precisely so the two scales cannot be confused.

A route is the same data, read the other way

Picture the incidence matrix: segments across, strands down. The drawing is built by reading columns — which strands does this segment gather? A route is a row — which segments held this strand? Nothing is added and nothing discarded. The matrix is transposed.

That transposition is the whole idea. A segment organises powerfully but only locally, at one x. A route organises along a strand across every x, which is what following anything requires.

02

Membership is read, then checked

Routes are only as good as the claim about which strands traverse which segments, and that claim could not be taken from PGB's data. This document is one PGB node, resolved by the server into 274 segments PGB has no names for. Membership had to come out of the drawing.

So it comes out of the drawing and is then checked against the drawing — the same redundancy doctrine parseSegmentBoxes.ts uses on a box's corners. Every quantity is read twice, by routes that share nothing, and the two readings must agree.

The check earned its place immediately. A first version read only the rectangles and matched 176 of 274. Every miss was an 18-unit box — the 1 bp variants — where the band is drawn as a bezier and never as a rect. Without the arithmetic that failure is invisible: the routes still look plausible, and every single-base variant is quietly absent from all of them.

Conservation. 40,442 band elements in the source, 40,442 across the 112 route files, 464 distinct strands, none duplicated.

At the 73 branch points, across all 464 strands, a strand took exactly one alternative 27,103 times and skipped the column entirely 6,769 times. It took two alternatives zero times. So a route is precisely the record of the choices a strand made: at each branch, this one, that one, or neither.

Route identity is correspondingly brittle. Route 4 is the consensus with one segment swapped for one other — nine strands do that, and it makes them a separate route. Two singleton routes can differ from each other by 2 segments out of 185. That brittleness is why seventy routes are singletons, and it is a property of the definition, not a defect in it.

04

The app

112 layers, one per route, stacked in register. The controls are the smallest set that makes the layers examinable.

route list

Every route as a row: index, a bar for how many strands take it, the count. The list is the distribution of Figure 3, sorted and clickable.

click to solo

Draws one route at full strength and recedes the other 111. Recede the others rather than brighten the one — spike story 30, applied to a route instead of a strand.

checkbox

Shows or hides any single layer independently, so arbitrary subsets can be composed by hand.

zoom

The document is 108,982 × 7,785. At fit a 15-unit band is under a pixel, so zoom is not a convenience — without it there is nothing to see. The readout gives band thickness in device pixels.

casing · shadow

The two depth treatments the essay recommends, wired to the whole stack so they can be looked at. Neither has been evaluated.

The result

The layers separate. Soloing a route leaves something legible where the composite is a solid mat, and arbitrary subsets stack back up in register with no transform. That is the assumption the depth proposal rests on, and it holds — for this document.

05

Two things the proposal assumes that this document does not support

1 · Cardinality is not a proxy for divergence here

The essay ranks divergence from consensus first and route cardinality second, justifying the second as broadly correlated with the first — cheap, and close enough. Over these 112 routes:

spearman(cardinality, divergence) = +0.104   p = 0.27

No relationship. The two orderings produce materially different stacks, so the choice between them is not the cheap one it appears to be.

2 · Jaccard conflates different with absent

Five routes carrying 95 strands — a fifth of the window — sit below J = 0.3. None is a divergent path. All five traverse between 5 and 12 of the 274 segments against the consensus route's 179: these are strands that barely cross the window. The largest carries 70 strands, more than any route but the consensus.

Ordering depth by divergence brings that route to the very front and reads it as the most unusual path in the picture. It is not unusual; it is absent. And opacity is already spent on exactly that distinction, so depth would assert unusual over strands the map is simultaneously greying as not placed.

This is a gap in the ranking, not a defect in it: whichever ordering is chosen, divergence has to be measured over the segments a strand could have traversed, not over the window's full segment set.

One window. Every number here describes 5520+. Whether either finding generalises is unmeasured.

06

The wrong turn, kept on the record

The first version bundled the 112 routes into five depth strata by carrier count, following the essay's argument that readers resolve five to seven shadow layers and 112 planes is not a thing anyone can see.

That reasoning is sound for a depth cue and wrong here. This app exists to look at routes as separable objects; bundling ninety-six of them onto one shelf delivered them pre-glued, back into the undifferentiated mat the split was made to break up.

The binning could not have worked at this locus under any rule. Seventy of the 112 routes are carried by a single strand, so any grouping by popularity must put all seventy on one shelf. The bottom stratum could never have held fewer than seventy routes. Tuning the edges moved it from 108 to 96 — noise, on a thing that was structurally stuck.

Strata are gone. Quantising is the renderer's problem, to be solved against a rendered picture, not baked into the file layout upstream of it. A route is the object; the layer is the object; they are one to one.

07

What it does not do

  • No depth cue is evaluated. Casing and shadow are wired up and both do something. Whether either reads as depth at these band widths is a perceptual question this app does not answer.
  • Nothing renders in the viewer. These are offline SVGs. src/tubemap/ is untouched, and no route computation exists in PGB.
  • Depth surviving a pan is untested. The essay's third caveat is a property of live route computation, which does not exist.
  • One document, one node. Routes here are structure inside 5520+. Nothing about this says anything about routes across nodes, which is the scale the panning caveat is actually about.
