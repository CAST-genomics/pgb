Depth Cueing for
Route Disambiguation

A response to the proposal that routes be ordered in depth so that drop shadowing or depth cueing can tease them apart. The proposal is sound, and for a sharper reason than it first appears: routes are the only level in this data at which a stable depth order exists at all.

The claim

Yes — routes can be ordered in depth, and depth is a better fit for routes than for anything else in the picture. But the argument that makes it work is a structural one, not an aesthetic one, and it is worth stating precisely because it is not obvious.

A depth cue only reads as depth if the ordering is globally consistent across the whole width of the picture. If strand A sits above B at one node and below B at the next, drop shadows do not disambiguate anything — they produce a Necker cube, and the reader's depth perception fights the data instead of serving it.

Ask which objects in the tube map can carry a consistent order:

  • Individual ribbons cannot. Their vertical positions come from the tube map's crossing-minimisation layout, which reorders them at every branch by design. There is no persistent "ribbon 12 is behind ribbon 7".
  • Nodes cannot. A node is not an object that persists across the window; it is a station, not a line.
  • A route can. A route is a set of nodes, invariant over the window. It is an atom. One scalar z per route is therefore well-defined and cannot self-contradict, and every ribbon on that route inherits it.

That is the load-bearing property. Depth ordering is not a rendering trick bolted onto the picture — it is a direct consequence of the survey's §01 finding that the picture's atoms are currently the wrong objects. Adopt routes as atoms and a depth channel falls out; keep ribbons as atoms and no depth channel is available at all.

Corollary. If a future change makes route identity unstable across the visible window, it does not merely degrade the depth cue — it invalidates it. See §04 caveat 3.

01

What to order by

Four candidate orderings, ranked. The first two are worth building; the second two look attractive and should be refused, for reasons that matter more than the ordering itself.

1

Divergence from the consensus route

Jaccard distance of each route's node set to the dominant route. Depth then means how unusual is this path — monotone, legible, and independent of how many haplotypes happen to carry it. The consensus route sits at the back as ground; deviants come forward as figure. This is the one to build.

2

Route cardinality

Haplotype count per route — simpler, already counted in the survey, and per §01 broadly correlated with divergence. Back-to-front is common-to-rare: the 84% route becomes backdrop, the singletons become foreground. Same figure/ground polarity, and it matches the survey's own conclusion that the signal lives in the tail.

AMI of 0.02–0.19 says route and PCLAI cluster are close to independent. Ordering depth by cluster would place unrelated paths in a shared plane and assert a relationship the data does not support. Ancestry already owns hue; keep the channels orthogonal rather than encoding one variable twice and a second variable never.

Placement quality — refuse

The confidence field is a warning channel with a duty attached (§05 of the survey). Burying impainted, low-confidence and unplaced strands at the back of the stack is exactly backwards: those are the strands a reader most needs brought forward.

02

Which cue, and why most are already spent

This matters more than it sounds. The tube map's perceptual channels are largely committed already, and a depth cue that borrows a committed channel destroys the thing it was borrowed from.

ChannelStatusVerdict
opacityspent — grey / absent placementunavailable
saturationspent — hue is the PCLAI payloadunavailable
thicknessreserved — would become a new claimdo not spend here
blurfree, but costs edge legibility at 1–2 px strandsweak
casing + drop shadowfreerecommended

Casing — drawing each ribbon with a thin background-coloured stroke behind it — reads unambiguously as over/under and consumes no colour, alpha, or width channel. It is what makes the London Underground map legible where lines bundle, and it is the cheap 80% of this proposal: pure paint order plus one extra stroke. A soft offset shadow on top of casing adds the stratum reading.

03

Strata, not a ramp

Twenty-one routes must not become twenty-one depth planes. Readers resolve perhaps five to seven shadow layers; beyond that a continuous depth ramp is read as continuous quantity, which is a precision claim the ordering does not support. Quantise.

Why depth is the safest new channel

Depth is ordinal only. Unlike thickness, it asserts no quantity, so adding it does not change what the map claims — it changes only what is easy to see. That makes it the cheapest of the survey's proposals to get wrong and recover from, and a reasonable thing to try before spending the thickness channel.

04

Three caveats that will bite

1 · chr6 degenerates, and should be allowed to

198 routes, none above 3%, 98 of them singletons. Every route falls into the rare stratum, so depth encodes nothing — the stack flattens. Do not paper over this with a finer-grained ramp. Fall back to a three-band singleton / rare / shared split and let the locus character badge say so out loud. This is consistent with the survey's own line that a locus where grouping buys nothing is itself a finding to hand the researcher.

2 · Quantisation boundaries are a claim

Where the bins fall decides what looks rare. Bin edges should be derived from the locus's own distribution and stated in the UI, not hard-coded — otherwise the picture changes character between loci for reasons that live in the code rather than in the data.

3 · Depth must survive panning

Route identity is scale- and window-dependent — the survey's method section warns that any affordance built on routes has to state at what scale it means "the same". If z is recomputed per viewport, ribbons will pop between strata as the reader scrolls, which is strictly worse than no depth at all, because a moving depth cue reads as motion. Either route IDs are stable across the pan range, or depth is locked to the window the reader is examining and annotated as such.

The order of operations. All three caveats are about route computation, not about rendering. Depth cueing cannot be prototyped ahead of a stable route identity — which the survey already names as the first question, not the last.

05

Where this touches PGB proper

The tube map panel owns its own scene graph and is deliberately not a Look (docs/adr/0001-sequence-tube-map-panel.md), so casing and shadows there are a panel-local rendering concern and nothing in the Look system needs to move.

But the route stratum is a datum, not a rendering detail. Computed once, the same ordinal is usable on both sides of the 1D ↔ 3D correspondence: as shadow depth in the tube map panel, and in the 3D graph as an emphasis parameter on NodeEmphasisLook — a new parameter on an existing Look, which is the shape the project's conventions ask for, not a new mechanism.

Open question, flagged rather than assumed

Routes are a haplotype-level grouping; the 3D graph's emphasis targets are nodes. A node carrying five routes across three strata has no single depth. Reconciling that — pick the nearest stratum, split the node, or scope the cue to the panel only — has to be answered before any of this reaches a Look. It is a genuine design question, not a detail.
